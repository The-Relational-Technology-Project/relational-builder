46th Ave, Moraga to Noriega: About 39 feet above sea level, 0.2 miles from Ocean Beach. Homes here were typically built around 1941; the oldest on the city's rolls is from 1908, the newest from 2004 (44 counted).

History pinned on or near it:
- Freedom House and Rainbow House (1941-1942), about 950 ft east: As World War II approached, Sunset builders gave their model homes patriotic or hopeful names. Henry Doelger's Rainbow House on 42nd Avenue dates to 1941, and his Freedom House on 43rd Avenue opened in 1942, both between Moraga and Noriega. They were among the last, because wartime rules soon halted nonessential building. - The Noriega seawall and its sand drifts (1980s, 2023), about 950 ft southwest: The seawall and promenade that start at Noriega date from the 1980s; planners label it the Noriega seawall. Wind piles sand against and over it every year. In June 2023 Public Works crews moved about 30,000 cubic yards of sand at the seawall between Noriega and Santiago, pushing it back toward the ocean. - The King Philip, a clipper ship under the sand (1878, 1902), about 0.3 mi southwest: On January 25, 1878, the 182 foot clipper King Philip lost her anchor hold and drifted onto Ocean Beach. Her lower hull is still there, buried on the beach near the foot of Noriega and Ortega. In 1902 the schooner Reporter wrecked on the same spot, and her timbers are mixed in. That is in the ocean due west of about Ortega and Pacheco streets, a little over 2 miles off the beach. The rupture began there at 5:12 a.m. on April 18 and ran along 296 miles of the San Andreas Fault.
