The neighborhood's own commons has already gathered some great material for this: the exact street cleaning windows for your block (East side Mondays, West side Thursdays) and a collection of local history—from the 1906 earthquake's starting point to the buried hull of the clipper ship *King Philip* just down the street.

Because this is for a single block, it's a perfect opportunity to connect with people already doing this work. Sandi Lam Harder and Josh Nesbit, who organize [The Sunset Wave](/gallery?item=repo:events:2027-03-20-the-sunset-wave), are wonderful resources for how to share a tool like this with your neighbors so it actually gets used.

Depending on how you want neighbors to interact with this, we could take a few different directions:

*   **The "Quick Check" Page**: A simple, bookmarkable URL. A neighbor opens it and sees a big "YES" or "NO" for today's cleaning, paired with a rotating history fact and one of the old block photos from OpenSFHistory.
*   **The "Reminder Hub"**: A way for neighbors to sign up for a text or email alert. The "hook" is the reminder, but the "gift" is the history fact delivered in the notification.
*   **The "Block Story-Wall"**: A visual gallery of the block's history that happens to have the cleaning schedule as the primary utility that brings people back every week.
