Good news: the two halves of this already exist, and one of them is a neighbor.

**The schedule is real and block-specific.** The city publishes sweeping for your exact stretch — 46th Ave between Moraga and Noriega: east side Monday, 1st & 3rd, noon–2; west side Thursday, 1st, 3rd & 5th, noon–2, and not on city holidays. The neighborhood notes already keep both the [street cleaning schedule](https://sfpublicworks.org/services/mechanical-street-sweeping-and-street-cleaning-schedule) and the [holiday enforcement list](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/drive-park/holiday-enforcement-schedule), so the tool reads the real thing instead of a hardcoded guess — and the signs on the block stay the last word.

**The history is already written down, pinned to your block.** Henry Doelger's Freedom House and Rainbow House on 42nd and 43rd (1941–42), the Noriega seawall and the 30,000 cubic yards of sand Public Works pushes back each June, the clipper *King Philip* buried off the foot of Noriega since 1878. The app can read those live with sources credited, and there are four old photographs from OpenSFHistory taken near here.

**Who to talk to first: The Sunset Wave.** Sandi Lam Harder and Josh Nesbit are organizing neighbor gatherings on blocks all over the Sunset on Saturday, March 20, 2027 — aiming for 400 of them. A street-cleaning reminder that arrives with a fact about your own block is exactly the thing you hand to the person next to you at a driveway coffee. Worth an email to oursunsetsocialclub@gmail.com before you build; they may already know someone on 46th. This is the same move as [The Claypole Commons](/gallery?item=kb_story%3A7f6a4286-e9b8-4f90-ab43-45ac834e0a0f) — a print newsletter for 25 houses on one block, about the cardinals and goldenrods they share rather than the people. The fact is the doorway; the sweeping day is the excuse.

## Question for you
1. How should the reminder actually reach you?
   - A page I open — I check it before I park
   - A calendar feed — sweeping days land in my own calendar app and ping me there
   - An email the night before
2. Who is it for?
   - Just me
   - Me and a few households on the block
   - The whole block, anyone between Moraga and Noriega
3. Which of these does it look like?
   - The seawall sign — weathered marine plywood bolted above the sand: ground #E7E1D3, ink #23241F, signal #C25A2B (rusted bolt orange), heavy condensed sans headline over a plain serif, square corners, airy
   - A 1941 builder's brochure — the pamphlet Doelger handed out for his model homes: cream #F6F1E4, ink #1E2A38, signal #A8322A (patriotic red), geometric sans with a slab serif, soft edges, dense
   - A tide chart — the harbor table pinned by the door: cool white #F7F8F5, ink #14324C, signal #2E7D8F (tidewater teal), monospace numerals for the times, hairline rules, square
