A block reminder fits what neighbors already use. The city's own [street sweeping schedule](https://sfpublicworks.org/services/mechanical-street-sweeping-and-street-cleaning-schedule) is the source to build from, and the reminder should link back to it. The block's schedule is already set: the east side is cleaned Mondays on the 1st and 3rd, and the west side Thursdays on the 1st, 3rd, and 5th, noon to 2pm. The signs on the block still have the final word.

Two neighbors to talk to before you build: [The Sunset Wave](https://thesunsetwave.org), organized by the Sunset Social Club, is already hosting block gatherings, and they can help you gather neighbors on 46th. The [Sunset Board](https://sunsetboard.org/add) carries flyers for the neighborhood. For the history facts, the commons has material pinned to this block: the Freedom House and Rainbow House builds from 1941 to 1942, and the Noriega seawall's sand drifts. Each fact carries its source, and I'd rather you check them than trust my summary.

The closest model is the [Claypole Commons](/gallery?item=kb_story%3A7f6a4286-e9b8-4f90-ab43-45ac834e0a0f), a hand-designed print newsletter for one block in Cincinnati that focuses on the land the neighbors share. Your reminder could work the same way, with the history fact as the thing that makes people read it.
