The city already publishes exactly what you'd be reminding people about. For 46th Ave between Moraga and Noriega: east side swept Mon 1st & 3rd, noon–2pm; west side Thu 1st, 3rd, 5th, noon–2pm — and not on city holidays. So the tool shouldn't invent a schedule, it should read the city's, live, and put it where neighbors actually look. The block's own commons carries that schedule alongside the history, at a block endpoint the app can read on load.

The history is real and it's good. Henry Doelger's Freedom House (43rd, 1942) and Rainbow House (42nd, 1941) — model homes given patriotic names just as wartime rules halted building. The clipper *King Philip*, wrecked on Ocean Beach in 1878, her ribs still buried near the foot of Noriega and surfacing every few years. The Noriega seawall and the ~30,000 cubic yards of sand Public Works pushes back each June. The 1906 earthquake's epicenter, straight out from Ortega. Old photographs from OpenSFHistory. Each reminder can carry one of these, sourced and credited on screen.

Who to talk to first: **The Sunset Wave** — Sandi Lam Harder and Josh Nesbit are organizing neighbor gatherings on every block in the Sunset for March 20, 2027, and 46th Ave is a block. A street-cleaning reminder is a fine excuse to knock on a door, and a tool made *with* them lands where one made around them duplicates. [Sunset Board](https://sunsetboard.org/add) is where flyers on poles get logged, if you go print.

Three shapes this could take:
- **A page you check** — one screen, both sides of the street, the next cleaning date and one true fact. Bookmark it, no signup.
- **A nudge that comes to you** — neighbors leave an email or number, and the day before each sweep they get a short message with the date and a fact.
- **A print sheet** — a monthly flyer for the door or the corkboard, dates and a fact, no tech at all.
