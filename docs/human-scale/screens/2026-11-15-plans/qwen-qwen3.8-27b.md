The block's own commons already holds the raw material for this. The city's sweeping schedule for 46th between Moraga and Noriega is published (east side: 1st & 3rd Mondays, west side: 1st, 3rd & 5th Thursdays, noon to 2, not on holidays), and there's real history pinned right here — the Freedom House and Rainbow House, Doelger's last model homes before wartime halted building in 1942; the King Philip clipper, her ribs still surfacing in the sand at the foot of Noriega after storms; the 1906 earthquake's starting point in the ocean due west of Ortega. Old photos from OpenSFHistory are linked and credited. That's enough for a good first rotation of facts without inventing anything.

Two people worth talking to before or while you build: **Sandi Lam Harder** and **Josh Nesbit**, who steward [The Sunset Wave](https://thesunsetwave.org) — neighbor gatherings on every block in the Sunset on March 20, 2027. That's a natural moment to introduce a block-level tool like this, and they already know how to get a block's attention. The [Sunset Board](https://sunsetboard.org/add) is also where flyers get logged, so a physical reminder on the pole is a companion move.

## Question for you
1. How does the reminder reach a neighbor on street cleaning day?
   - A simple page they bookmark — they open it in the morning and see today's reminder + fact
   - A calendar subscription (.ics) — it lands in their phone calendar automatically, no app to open
   - A post to a block group chat — you (or a neighbor) share it the night before
   - All of the above, but start with one — which one first?

2. Which of these does it look like?
   - The yellow "Street Cleaning" sign on the pole — pale yellow ground `#F5E6A3`, black ink, one red `#C0392B` for the date, chunky sans-serif (Archivo Black + Source Sans), square edges, dense
   - A nautical log from the clipper era — deep ocean blue ground `#1B2A4A`, cream text `#F0EAD6`, one sand-gold `#C9A96E` for the fact, serif display (Playfair Display) + clean body (Inter), soft edges, airy
   - A 1940s model home brochure — warm cream ground `#FAF5EB`, dark brown ink `#3D2B1F`, one period teal `#2E7D6B` for the fact, geometric sans (DM Sans) + a warm serif for the fact (Lora), soft edges, medium density
